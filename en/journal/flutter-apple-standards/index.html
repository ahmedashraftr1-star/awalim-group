    Flutter and Apple's standards: building an experience at the level of official iOS

    The differences a user notices without naming them: motion curves, scroll bounce and touch timing — and where they come from when they are not native.

    By Awalim mobile team

    In this article

    1. 1. Motion curves
    2. 2. Scroll bounce
    3. 3. Touch timing
    4. 4. Typography and Arabic letterforms
    5. 5. Safe areas and direction
    6. In short

    An iPhone user does not say «this app is not native». They say «something feels off» and delete it. That something is usually one of five details that can be measured — and all of them are fixable in Flutter if you know where to look.

    1. Motion curves

    iOS does not use the standard ease-in-out. Screen transitions use a spring curve with a specific damping, and menus open on a different curve than buttons. Flutter ships with Curves.easeInOut by default, and that is the first thing that feels wrong.

    // instead of Curves.easeInOut — a curve closer to UIKit's behaviour
final iosCurve = Cubic(0.25, 0.1, 0.25, 1.0);

// and for screen transitions: CupertinoPageRoute, not MaterialPageRoute
Navigator.of(context).push(
  CupertinoPageRoute(builder: (_) => const DetailsScreen()),
);

    2. Scroll bounce

    Lists in iOS bounce at the edge and decelerate with a specific friction coefficient. BouncingScrollPhysics exists in Flutter but is not applied to every list unless you set ScrollBehavior at the app level. Set it once at the root, not in every list.

    3. Touch timing

    A button in iOS responds on release, not on press, and cancels if the finger moves beyond a certain distance. CupertinoButton does this; ElevatedButton does not. The difference is felt in the first second of use.

    4. Typography and Arabic letterforms

    SF Pro is the system font and Flutter cannot ship it. But using .SF Pro Text through the platform's default font family works on iOS. Arabic, though, needs a font you ship yourself — and we use IBM Plex Sans Arabic at its real weights, not one weight faked with a stroke.

    • Never apply letterSpacing to Arabic text — it severs the joins between letters
    • A taller line height for Arabic (1.6 and up), because diacritics and extenders need the room
    • Tabular figures (tabularFigures) in any column of numbers

    5. Safe areas and direction

    An Arabic screen mirrors: back is on the right, and drawers pull from the right. Flutter mirrors automatically if you use EdgeInsetsDirectional and AlignmentDirectional — and breaks the app if you use explicit left/right. The rule: no word «left» or «right» in the code without a written reason.

    A native app is not the one that uses the system's widgets, but the one that behaves the way the system's user expects — on the first touch, the first scroll, and the first transition.

    In short

    Flutter is capable of an experience at the level of official iOS, but it does not hand it to you by default. Five decisions — the curve, the physics, the touch, the typography, the direction — are taken once at the root of the app, and everything after inherits the right feel.
